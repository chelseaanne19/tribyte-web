# User research, prototype testing and honest metrics

The goal is to discover real needs and usability problems, not to get compliments. Separate **contributors** (people who know Dublin) from **readers** (newer arrivals who need help).

## A. Collect recommendations from experienced students

Aim to speak with **4–6** current/informed international or exchange students. Ask about actual recent behaviour:

1. What was confusing when you first arrived in Dublin?
2. Which grocery/transport/budgeting advice did you actually use?
3. Can you recall one affordable place, study spot or day out you'd genuinely recommend?
4. How did you get there? What did you pay approximately *at the time*?
5. What tip have you already shared with another student?
6. What information might be outdated soon?

Get explicit permission before publishing words, photos or a public attribution; let contributors choose a pseudonym or anonymous label. Verify locations and important factual claims. The initial **12–18 tips** are a target, not fictitious seed data.

## B. Test the guide with newcomers (R1 pilot)

Recruit around **five** newer international/exchange students, preferably with varied familiarity with UCD, housing areas and devices. Avoid giving participants the exact answer during testing.

**Tasks (ask in natural language):**

1. 'Find a useful grocery-shopping recommendation for a student living near UCD.'
2. 'Find a tip about using Dublin public transport or understanding how to get around.'
3. 'Find an affordable meal or café that sounds suitable for you.'
4. 'Find a place to study or something to do on a free afternoon.'
5. 'Tell me whether you trust this tip and what information is missing.'

**Observe:** task completion unassisted, time only if helpful, navigation path, confusion, usefulness and trust; whether general tips are findable even without a map.

**Suggested learning goal:** at least **4/5** can find an appropriate tip without help; this is a pilot target, not statistically significant proof of usefulness.

## C. Test map only after guide (R2)

Ask users to locate a genuine place recommendation using the **list**, then using the **map** (vary the order among participants). Record which is faster/easier for that particular task. Don't assume every category benefits from pins.

## D. Test the postcard after one template works (R3)

'Create a postcard from a photo you're comfortable choosing, and save the result.' Don't coach. Record whether the user completes unaided and gets a valid PNG. Ask: 'What would you do with it now?' rather than suggesting sharing. Avoid collecting personal photo files.

## E. Test the physical souvenir independently

Show the physical prototype **before** mentioning the QR experience. Ask about prior souvenir purchases and perceived usefulness/value. Then explain the digital companion and see whether interest changes. Stated willingness-to-pay does **not** equal actual sales.

## Pitch and portfolio evidence

Track exact numbers, test scripts, screenshots of version differences, and anonymised observations. Do not claim 'X active users' from interviews or QR scans alone. Keep sensitive research notes out of a public GitHub repository.
